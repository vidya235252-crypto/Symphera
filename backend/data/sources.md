# Sources

[S1] WHO PCOS fact sheet | https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/polycystic-ovary-syndrome
- Common features include irregular periods, signs of excess androgens (such as acne and excess hair), and polycystic ovaries.
- PCOS affects a significant share of women of reproductive age and is often underdiagnosed.
- Management is lifestyle-based and clinician-guided.

[S2] Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) | American Society for Reproductive Medicine (ReproductiveFacts.org) | https://integration.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/polycystic-ovary-syndrome-pcos/
- Patient fact sheet covering symptoms, diagnosis, health risks, and treatment options.
- The page says it was revised in 2014; use it as background and check current clinical guidance for present-day recommendations.

[S3] Polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS) | Gloucestershire Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust | https://www.gloshospitals.nhs.uk/your-visit/patient-information-leaflets/polycystic-ovarian-syndrome-pcos-ghpi0772_11_18/
- Patient leaflet explaining symptoms, tests, the difference between PCO and PCOS, and management.
- The page links to a leaflet reviewed in April 2024, with review due April 2027.

[S4] Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) | Eunice Kennedy Shriver National Institute of Child Health and Human Development (NICHD) | https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/pcos
- U.S. government health information covering PCOS features, symptoms, diagnosis, and research.
- Describes how symptoms can affect menstrual, reproductive, metabolic, and other aspects of health.

[S5] The Ultimate Guide to PCOS and PCOD | Pinkishe Foundation | https://pinkishe.org/blog-post/the-ultimate-guide-to-pcos-and-pcod-everything-you-need-to-know-about-polycystic-ovary-syndrome-and-polycystic-ovarian-disease
- General-audience overview of PCOS/PCOD.
- Treat as a secondary source; verify medical claims against government health agencies, professional societies, or current clinical guidelines.

[S6] PCOS Symptoms: When to Visit a Doctor | Jacob Pediatrics and Family Medicine | https://jacobpediatrics.com/pcos-symptoms-when-to-visit-a-doctor/
- Clinic blog discussing possible symptoms, seeking care, diagnosis, and management.
- It includes promotional clinic content; use it as a secondary source and cross-check clinical claims.

[S7] Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) | Cleveland Clinic | https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/8316-polycystic-ovary-syndrome-pcos
- Clinician-reviewed patient overview of symptoms, diagnosis, and treatment.
- Useful for clear explanations of common symptoms and when to seek medical advice.

[S8] PCOS Symptoms and Treatment | Chetna Hospital | https://chetnahospital.co.in/pcos-symptoms-and-treatment-chinchwad/
- Hospital information page about PCOS symptoms and treatment.
- The page could not be retrieved during review; confirm that it is accessible and current before relying on it.

[S9] PCOS Symptoms: Early Signs That Should Not Be Ignored | Magrabi Health | https://www.magrabihealth.com/blog/pcos-symptoms
- Health-provider blog describing common PCOS symptoms.
- Use as a secondary explainer and verify clinical statements with higher-authority medical sources.

[S10] A Patient’s Guide: Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) | The Endocrine Society, via PubMed Central | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5393469/
- Patient guide covering symptoms, similar conditions, diagnosis, follow-up tests, and treatment.
- Published in 2014 and based on the Endocrine Society’s 2013 clinical practice guideline; useful background, but not a substitute for current guidance.

## Notes for the Women’s Health – Understanding Symptoms project

- Strong starting references from this list: WHO, NICHD, Cleveland Clinic, and the Gloucestershire Hospitals NHS patient leaflet.
- Use ASRM and the Endocrine Society guide with their publication dates in mind. Treat Pinkishe, Jacob Pediatrics, Chetna Hospital, and Magrabi Health as supplementary sources and cross-check their medical claims.
- Present symptom information as educational guidance that can help someone decide what to discuss with a qualified clinician. Do not present it as a diagnosis.
- For regional-language inputs, preserve the original wording alongside any translated or normalized interpretation, and ground explanations in trusted medical sources.
